## pag 97 n1
1.    Late Victorian novel → mirrored society’s moral and religious crisis, influenced by Social Darwinism.
2.    Carroll’s Wonderland → illogical, irrational, chaotic, questioning Victorian certainties.
3.    Crime fiction → popular due to rising crime anxieties and the new role of detectives as heroes of order.
4.    Jekyll and Hyde → crime novel because of the detective figure and hidden crimes/dualities.
5.    Hardy’s novels → realistic, psychological, pessimistic, with outsiders against narrow society.
6.    Kipling → set in colonies, used imperial realities as backdrop for adventurous stories

## pag 135 n4 

1. **What does the speaker sing? How does he relate to the people he meets?**  
   The speaker sings about the human body and its beauty. He connects deeply with people, feeling their bodies and souls are equal and sacred.

2. **What does he believe?**  
   He believes the body is as important as the soul and that all bodies are divine and connected.

3. **Whose bodies does he celebrate?**  
   He celebrates all bodies—men, women, young, old, free, and enslaved.

4. **What parts of the body does he sing?**  
   He sings about the whole body, including limbs, heart, eyes, and more, praising their strength and beauty.

5. **What realization is very pleasant?**  
   It is pleasant to realize that the body is sacred and connected to the soul, making every person divine.

6. **What conclusion does the speaker draw?**  
   The speaker concludes that all bodies are equal, holy, and part of a universal connection, worthy of love and celebration.
